About Disk Scheduling

Disk scheduling is the method an operating system uses to decide the order in which pending disk I/O requests are served. The goal is to reduce seek time, the time the read/write head spends moving between cylinders.

⚡

Why it matters

Less head movement means faster response, higher throughput and less wear on the disk.

⚖️

Fairness

A good algorithm avoids starvation, where some requests wait too long.

Disk Scheduling Algorithms

FCFS

First Come First Serve

Serves requests exactly in arrival order. Simple and fair, but the head may swing back and forth a lot.

SSTF

Shortest Seek Time First

Always picks the request closest to the current head position. Low movement, but far requests may starve.

SCAN

Elevator Algorithm

Head moves in one direction to the disk end, serving requests on the way, then reverses.

C-SCAN

Circular SCAN

Like SCAN, but after reaching one end the head jumps to the other end and keeps scanning in the same direction.

LOOK

LOOK

Like SCAN, but the head only goes as far as the last request in each direction, not to the disk end.

C-LOOK

Circular LOOK

Like C-SCAN, but jumps from the last request to the first request on the other side.

    Key terms

    • Cylinder: a track position on the disk.
    • Seek time: time to move the head to a cylinder.
    • Head movement: total cylinders crossed.
    • Starvation: a request waits indefinitely.

    FCFS

    • + Simple, fair
    • − High head movement

    SSTF

    • + Low average seek time
    • − Can starve far requests

    SCAN / C-SCAN

    • + No starvation, good under heavy load
    • − Travels to disk ends; C-SCAN adds a jump

    LOOK / C-LOOK

    • + Avoids useless trips to the ends
    • − Slightly more complex

    Frequently asked questions

    Which algorithm is always the best?

    None. It depends on the request pattern. SSTF or LOOK often win on random loads, while C-SCAN and C-LOOK give more uniform waiting times.

    What is the difference between SCAN and LOOK?

    SCAN always travels to the end of the disk. LOOK turns around at the last request.

    Why do the circular versions exist?

    They serve requests in only one direction, so waiting time is more uniform for requests at the edges.

    How is total head movement calculated?

    It is the sum of absolute differences between consecutive cylinders in the seek sequence.
